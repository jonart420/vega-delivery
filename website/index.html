About

Built for the people who load the van at 7 am

Most delivery software is either a spreadsheet with a map or an enterprise suite with a sales team. VEGA Delivery sits in between: one Docker Compose file, your own server, your own data, and every piece of the day covered – from typing in a new customer to the signature at the door.

The dispatcher keeps customers, products and orders, plans the orders of a day into tours and lets the routing engine find the fastest order of stops for a car. The driver signs in to the app by scanning a QR code, starts the tour and navigates stop by stop. Every outcome – delivered, left with a neighbour, refused with a reason – is logged against the customer and the tour.

Customers are not left guessing: when the tour starts they get a message that their delivery arrives today, and when the van is three stops away, they get a live tracking link.

Self-hosted

Docker Compose with PostgreSQL, Valhalla and the back office. No tracking of your customers by a third party.

Open source

AGPL-3.0. If someone offers a modified version as a service, they have to publish their changes.

Any region

Routing data from OpenStreetMap: a city, a country or the whole planet, set in one variable.

Two languages

English and German throughout – back office, messages, tracking page and both apps – with full Unicode support.

The product

How a delivery day runs

The steps below happen in this order on every tour. Each one is a screen or an automatic action in VEGA Delivery.

  1. Orders come inCreate orders for customers from the address book, with items from your product catalogue, a delivery date and an optional time window.
  2. The dispatcher plans toursOpen orders of a day go into a tour. Several orders of one customer become one stop. Optimize route finds the fastest order for a car.
  3. Release to the driverAssign a driver and release the tour. Print the loading list and labels.
  4. The driver starts the tourCustomers get "your delivery arrives today". The first three stops get a live tracking link right away.
  5. Stop by stopThe app navigates by car. On arrival, the driver taps the outcome and adds the required proof: signature, photo, recipient name or a barcode scan.
  6. The window moves onWith every completed stop the next customer enters the three-stop window and receives the tracking link.
  7. Close the tourStops that could not be reached go back to open orders for re-planning. The full log stays with each customer.

Address book

Customers with geocoded addresses, a draggable delivery pin, delivery notes, time windows, contact data and notification consent. CSV import and export.

Products & orders

A category tree and products with SKU, unit, price, VAT and EAN-13. Orders keep a snapshot of every item, so editing a product never changes history.

Car-optimized tours

Exact optimization up to 13 stops, local search (2-opt, Or-opt) above, soft time windows, pinned positions and manual reordering.

Outcomes you define

Delivered, left with neighbour, safe place, nobody home, refused, aborted – and any outcome you add, each with its own required proofs and reasons.

Tracking link

A page per stop. The vehicle position appears only once the customer is among the next stops – nobody sees where the van was an hour ago.

E-mail & WhatsApp

SMTP for e-mail, the WhatsApp Cloud API or Twilio for WhatsApp. Templates per language, automatic and manual sending, every message logged.

Barcodes

Two codes, one for the parcel, one for the door

Every order carries an EAN-13 delivery code from the GS1 range for internal use. It can change with every delivery, or stay constant per customer for reusable crates.

Every customer has a customer indicator: an EAN-8 composite symbol (ISO/IEC 24723) with the customer number in the bars and the name in the 2D part above. Print it once on a one-page sheet and leave it at the delivery point. Scanning it at the door is the proof of delivery – no new label per order.

Linear part · EAN-8 5245165 + check digit 2 → 52451652 2D part · GS1 AI (91) (91)Herbert_M%C3%BCller_-_Strandleben_GmbH Decoded by the app Herbert Müller - Strandleben GmbH

GS1 character set 82 has neither spaces nor umlauts, so spaces become _ and other characters are UTF-8 percent-encoded – the symbol stays standard-compliant.

Setup

Running in about fifteen minutes

You need a Linux server or a Mac with Docker and Docker Compose, and about 5 GB of disk for a city or region. A whole country needs more – see routing regions.

1. Create the configuration

generate-env.sh copies .env.example to .env and fills every secret with a strong random value. It prints the first admin password.

2. Choose your region and channels

Edit .env: set VALHALLA_TILE_URLS to the OpenStreetMap extract of your area, and fill in SMTP_* and WHATSAPP_* if you want to notify customers.

3. Start

The first start downloads the map data and builds the routing tiles – minutes for a city, an hour or two for a country. The back office is usable right away; route optimization starts working once the build is done.

4. Put HTTPS in front

The back office listens on 127.0.0.1:3100. Use any reverse proxy with TLS and set PUBLIC_BASE_URL to your domain – it appears in tracking links, e-mails and the drivers' login QR codes.

# get the code and create .env with random secrets
git clone <your fork> vega-delivery
cd vega-delivery
./scripts/generate-env.sh https://delivery.example.com

# region, e-mail, WhatsApp, maps …
nano .env

# start database, routing engine and back office
docker compose up -d
docker compose logs -f valhalla

# optional: demo data (customers, orders, a tour)
cd backend
BASE_URL=http://localhost:3100 node scripts/seed-demo.mjs
# Caddy example for HTTPS
delivery.example.com {
  reverse_proxy 127.0.0.1:3100
}
The security gate

In production, the back office refuses to run while .env still holds example values (change-me…), empty secrets, passwords shorter than 16 characters or secrets shorter than 32 bytes. Every page then explains what to fix, the API answers 503, no admin is created and no message is sent. Fix .env and run docker compose up -d again.

Routing regions

A city extract builds in minutes with 2 GB of RAM; a German state in about 10 minutes; a whole country in one to two hours with 8–16 GB. The planet works too, but needs more than 64 GB of RAM and about a terabyte of disk. Update monthly with scripts/valhalla-update.sh.

WhatsApp

Messages a business sends first need a template approved by Meta or Twilio. Each template gets three variables: the customer name, the ETA and the tracking link. Set the template name per message kind, e.g. WHATSAPP_META_TEMPLATE_APPROACHING.

Branding

Replace logo.svg, logo.png and favicon.png in branding/. The folder is mounted into the container, so no rebuild is needed. The logo appears in the back office, on labels, in e-mails and on the tracking page.

Backups

scripts/db-backup.sh writes a compressed dump to backups/ and removes dumps older than 30 days. Also keep .env (above all LOGIN_CODE_SECRET) and the uploads volume with photos and signatures.

Updates

git pull, docker compose build backend, docker compose up -d. Database migrations run automatically when the back office starts.

Driver apps

Build the iOS app from ios/ with XcodeGen and Xcode, the Android app from android/ with Android Studio. Set your own bundle id, app name and default server in Config/Shared.xcconfig and gradle.properties.

Usage

Working in the back office

Admins and dispatchers work in the browser. The sidebar groups everything into Dispatch, Master data and Administration.

First steps after installing

  • Settings → General: company name, phone (shown on the tracking page) and the depot, which becomes start and end of new tours.
  • Team: add drivers. Each gets a personal QR login code to print or send. Dispatchers and admins also get a password for the back office.
  • Address book and Products: enter or import your customers and catalogue.
  • Settings → Notifications: check the channels, choose when messages go out and adjust the templates.

Address book

Search the address field to fill street, number, postcode and city in one go. Addresses are geocoded on save; drag the pin when the van has to stop at the back entrance. Delivery notes ("gate code 4711") show up in the app and on labels.

Turn on Notify by e-mail or Notify via WhatsApp only with the customer's consent – the time of consent is stored. Each customer can have its own language for messages and the tracking page.

The customer page also lists all orders and the full delivery history, including photos and signatures.

Orders and tours

Create an order from the customer page or under New order. Filter the order list by date and status, select open orders and plan them into a new or existing tour – or print their labels.

In the tour planner, Optimize route calculates the fastest order for a car. Use ▲ ▼ to change the order by hand and the pin to keep a stop at its position when you optimize again. Assign a driver and Release to driver. Changing stops afterwards sets the tour back to draft.

While the tour runs, the same page becomes a live view: stop states, the vehicle on the map, the delivery log and every message sent to customers.

Outcomes and reasons

The outcome buttons in the driver app come from Settings → Outcomes & reasons. For each outcome choose its type (success, failure or neutral), a colour, and which proofs it requires: reason, photo, signature, recipient name, barcode scan.

Add reasons per outcome, such as "Goods damaged" or "Address not found". Outcomes and reasons that were already used are deactivated instead of deleted, so the log stays complete.

Notifications

Set how many stops ahead the tracking link goes out (default: three), whether the day-start message is sent, and whether customers get a confirmation after delivery. Templates exist per message kind and language, with placeholders like {{eta}} and {{tracking_link}}. Send a test message before going live.

Driver app

Everything a driver needs, nothing else

Native apps for iOS (SwiftUI) and Android (Jetpack Compose), both with Ferrostar turn-by-turn navigation. The Android app is a basic version that has not been compiled yet.

Sign in by QR

The login code contains the server address, so drivers never type a URL. Lost phone? Revoke the code in Team and the device is signed out.

Navigation for cars

Voice instructions in the device language, arrival detection within 50 m, wrong-way warnings, Later to move a stop to the end and Re-optimize from the current position.

Works offline

Tours and route legs are stored on the phone. Recorded deliveries wait in an outbox and are sent when the network is back – never twice.

For your customers

A tracking link that respects privacy

Each stop gets its own link with a random 128-bit token. Before the van is close, the page shows the expected time and how many stops are ahead. Only within the last three stops does it show the vehicle on a map with a live ETA. After delivery it shows the result, and the link expires at the end of the tour day.

The page never shows other customers or the route of the van – only this delivery.
